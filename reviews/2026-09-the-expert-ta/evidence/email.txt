Why the template matters
The VPAT isn't a formatting preference on our end. As a CSU campus we are a Title II public entity, and our accessibility reviews are auditable records. What we have to be able to show an auditor is a complete Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR) on the ITI template, with a documented response for every criterion in scope — including the ones that don't apply, marked "Not Applicable" with a short reason. A blank row and a "Not Applicable" row look identical in an audit file: neither shows the criterion was evaluated. That's the entire reason an incomplete document can't be accepted, even a well-written one.
Your August 17 message noted that Expert TA is using the most recent template available. The gap is which edition. ITI publishes four editions of VPAT 2.5Rev and they cover different WCAG versions: the 508 edition covers WCAG 2.0, the EU edition WCAG 2.1, and the WCAG and INT editions WCAG 2.2. Please use the VPAT 2.5Rev INT edition — it carries WCAG plus the Revised Section 508 chapters in one document and covers everything we need. It's a free download from ITI at itic.org/policy/accessibility/vpat. We review to WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA, so Level AAA is not required — that table can be deleted entirely.
What's missing from the May 5 document
1. Nine WCAG Level A/AA criteria have no response. The document answers 41 of the 50 Level A and AA criteria. These nine have no row at all:
•	1.2.3 Audio Description or Media Alternative (Prerecorded) — Level A
•	1.2.5 Audio Description (Prerecorded) — Level AA
•	1.3.4 Orientation — Level AA
•	1.3.5 Identify Input Purpose — Level AA
•	1.4.10 Reflow — Level AA
•	1.4.13 Content on Hover or Focus — Level AA
•	2.5.1 Pointer Gestures — Level A
•	2.5.2 Pointer Cancellation — Level A
•	2.5.4 Motion Actuation — Level A
The 2.5.x criteria are the WCAG 2.1 pointer-input additions and are directly relevant to a platform with drag-and-drop and hotspot question types. 1.4.10 Reflow matters for students working on tablets. 1.2.3 and 1.2.5 can't be safely skipped given the instructional video library described in your August 17 message.
2. Three Section 508 chapters are absent entirely. The ACR needs Chapter 3 Functional Performance Criteria (302.1–302.9), Chapter 5 Software (501.1, 502.x, 503.x, 504.x), and Chapter 6 Support Documentation and Services (601–603.3). That's roughly 49 criteria with no response. Chapter 5 in particular includes 504.2 Content Creation or Editing, which applies because instructors author assignments in your platform, and Chapter 6 covers your help documentation and your support desk. Chapter 4 Hardware can be removed as not applicable, but the template requires a note in that section saying so rather than silent deletion.
3. Required report elements are missing. The ITI Essential Requirements list ten items that must appear in the header. The document has the report date and the author's name. Still needed: report title in the form "Expert TA Accessibility Conformance Report," the VPAT version statement "(Based on VPAT® Version 2.5Rev)" with the service mark, product name and version, product description, contact information, Notes, Applicable Standards/Guidelines, the Terms definitions, and — most important for us — Evaluation Methods Used.
Two notes on that list. On contact information, the ACR requires an email address for accessibility follow-up questions; the May 5 document is compiled by Jeremy Morton but lists no address, so please include the right contact for whoever should field those.
On Evaluation Methods, the testing description in your August 17 message is exactly what belongs in that section: seven developers and eight testers, JAWS, NVDA and VoiceOver, across Mac and PC in all major browsers, with no reliance on simulated or third-party automated reports. That's a stronger methodology statement than most ACRs we receive, and right now none of it appears in the document. Putting it in Evaluation Methods converts it from an email claim into part of the record we can rely on.
One substantive item, separate from completeness
The May 5 document answers every one of the 51 criteria as either "Supports" or "Not Applicable," and uses several terms that aren't in the template's vocabulary — "Platform Supports," "Content Supports with rare exceptions," "Supported with Exceptions." The template defines exactly five: Supports, Partially Supports, Does Not Support, Not Applicable, Not Evaluated. If you use different terms, they have to be defined in the Terms section.
More than a vocabulary issue: your own remarks under 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum) and 1.4.5 Images of Text describe known exceptions, and your August 17 message identifies three question types that are not compliant — drag-and-drop labeling, hotspot, and vector practice. Under the template's definitions those criteria are Partially Supports, not Supports. An ACR that says Partially Supports with a clear remark is a document our reviewers can act on; one that says Supports everywhere is one an auditor will question.
